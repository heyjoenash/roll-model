# Roll Model — Bowling Glossary

> **Last Updated**: 2026-04-13
> **Purpose**: Canonical definitions for every bowling term that appears in Roll Model content. When writing new sections, define jargon on first use AND keep definitions consistent with this glossary. When in doubt, this is the source of truth.

This is not a comprehensive bowling dictionary. It only includes terms that appear in our content map, interaction blueprints, or research source files. If a term isn't here, it shouldn't appear in production content (or should be added here first).

Terms are linked back to the section(s) where they're most prominently introduced.

---

## A

**Approach** — The runway behind the foul line where the bowler walks before releasing the ball. Standard length: 15 feet. Has marker dots at 12 and 15 feet from the foul line for positioning. *Introduced: Ch 3 sections.*

**Approach (the 4-step / 5-step)** — The pattern of footwork a bowler uses while delivering the ball. The 4-step is more common; the 5-step adds a "trigger step" at the start to build momentum. *Introduced: Ch 3.2, 3.3.*

**Arrows** — Seven dart-like markers painted on the lane 15 feet from the foul line, on boards 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, and 35. Used as targeting reference points instead of trying to look at the pins 60 feet away. *Introduced: Ch 1.1.*

**Arsenal** — A bowler's collection of bowling balls, each suited for different lane conditions. Tournament bowlers commonly carry 3-6 balls. *Introduced: Ch 10.2.*

**Axis** — The imaginary line through the bowling ball around which it spins after release. Determined by the bowler's hand position at release. Has two key properties: tilt and rotation. *Introduced: Ch 5.3, 5.4.*

**Axis rotation** — The angle between the ball's spin axis and its direction of travel. 0° = end-over-end roll (no hook potential). 90° = pure side spin (max hook potential). Most bowlers fall between 30° and 60°. *Introduced: Ch 5.4.*

**Axis tilt** — The angle of the ball's spin axis relative to the lane surface. 0° = the ball rolls flat like a car tire (max contact, max friction). 90° = the ball spins like a top (min contact, min friction). Most bowlers fall between 5° and 25°. *Introduced: Ch 5.3.*

## B

**Backswing** — The portion of the swing where the ball travels behind the bowler's body. Higher backswings store more potential energy and produce faster ball speed. Typical range: belt-high to above-head. *Introduced: Ch 4.2.*

**Ball motion** — How the ball travels down the lane. Conventionally divided into three phases: skid, hook, roll. The shape of ball motion depends on speed, rev rate, axis tilt/rotation, surface, and oil pattern. *Introduced: Ch 2.4, Ch 6.*

**Belly** — The widest part of a bowling pin, located about 4.5 inches from the base. The pin's max diameter is 4.766 inches at the belly. *Introduced: Ch 1.2.*

**Boards** — The 39 narrow longitudinal strips that make up the lane width. Each board is just over 1 inch wide. Numbered 1-39. For a right-handed bowler, board 1 is on the right (gutter side) and board 20 is the center. *Introduced: Ch 1.4.*

**Breakpoint** — The point on the lane where the ball changes direction most sharply — the moment the hook "kicks in." For a typical house shot, the breakpoint is around board 9-10 at about 40-45 feet from the foul line. *Introduced: Ch 6.3.*

**Brooklyn** — A strike (or shot) where a right-handed bowler's ball crosses to the left side of the head pin and enters the "wrong" pocket. Counts the same as a regular strike but is generally considered a lucky shot. *Introduced: Ch 8.1.*

---

## C

**Carrydown** — Oil that has been pushed down the lane past the original pattern length by ball travel. Causes the backends to become "less reactive" over the course of multiple games. *Introduced: Ch 7.5.*

**Center of gravity (CG)** — The balance point of the bowling ball. Marked with a small dot on the surface. Usually the geometric center for a symmetric core, slightly offset for asymmetric cores. *Introduced: Ch 2.6.*

**Cheetah** — A 35-foot PBA pattern. Short, with low oil ratio. Historically the easiest of the PBA animal patterns because outside boards are dry and forgiving. *Introduced: Ch 7.3.*

**Coverstock** — The outer surface material of the bowling ball. The five main types are plastic, urethane, reactive solid, reactive pearl, and reactive hybrid. The coverstock is the single biggest factor in how the ball moves. *Introduced: Ch 2.1.*

**Cranker** — A bowling style characterized by very high rev rate (400-550+ RPM) and aggressive hook. Crankers generate max revolutions through wrist action and a powerful release. Compare with stroker. *Introduced: Ch 5.2.*

**Cupped wrist** — A wrist position at release where the back of the hand is bent toward the forearm, "cupping" the ball. Generates the highest possible rev rate. Compare with broken wrist. *Introduced: Ch 5.1.*

---

## D

**Differential** — The difference between a bowling ball's max RG and min RG. Indicates how much the ball will track-flare during travel. USBC legal range: 0.010 to 0.060. Higher differential = more flare = more total hook. *Introduced: Ch 2.3.*

**Dragon** — A 45-foot PBA pattern. Long with a low ratio, requiring deeper inside lines. *Introduced: Ch 7.3.*

**Drift** — Lateral movement during the approach. Most bowlers drift slightly. Drift itself isn't a problem; INCONSISTENT drift is. Pros often drift 4-6 boards intentionally and consistently. *Introduced: Ch 3.5.*

---

## E

**Entry angle** — The angle at which the ball strikes the head pin, measured from the direction parallel to the boards. USBC research found the optimal angle is 6 degrees — at this angle the strike zone is roughly 3x wider than at 2 degrees. *Introduced: Ch 8.2.*

**Equator** — The widest circle on the bowling ball perpendicular to its spin axis. The semi-transparent stripe in our 3D ball visualization marks the equator to make rotation visible. *Internal term, used in scene descriptions.*

---

## F

**Finger holes** — The two holes in the bowling ball where the bowler's middle and ring fingers go. About 28mm in diameter. Together with the thumb hole, they form the "grip." *Introduced: Ch 2.6.*

**Flare (track flare)** — The visible rings of oil left on a ball's surface after it has rolled down the lane. Each ring marks where a different "track" of the ball touched the lane. High differential = more flare = more hook potential. *Introduced: Ch 2.3.*

**Flat 10** — A 10-pin leave where the 6-pin has been driven down weakly into the channel before reaching the 10-pin. Indicates the ball entered the pocket without enough energy. Compare with ringing 10. *Introduced: Ch 8.5.*

**Foul line** — The line at the start of the lane (60 feet from the pins). Stepping over it during a delivery results in a foul (zero pins counted). *Introduced: Ch 1.1.*

**Free swing** — A relaxed, gravity-driven pendulum swing where the bowler doesn't apply muscle force to the swing path. Produces the most consistent ball speed and release. Compare with muscled swing. *Introduced: Ch 4.4.*

---

## G

**Grit** — The roughness rating of a ball's surface, on a scale from ~500 (very rough sandpaper feel) to 5000+ (mirror polish). Grit dramatically affects ball motion: lower grit hooks earlier, higher grit hooks later. Surface adjustment is the cheapest, fastest way to change a ball's reaction. *Introduced: Ch 2.5.*

**Gutter** — The two channels alongside the lane where the ball goes when it misses the lane surface. Each is 9 inches wide. *Introduced: Ch 1.1.*

---

## H

**Hand (handedness)** — Right-handed or left-handed. Affects which side of the head pin the ball should enter (the "pocket"): right-handers aim for the 1-3 pocket, left-handers for the 1-2 pocket. *Universally referenced.*

**Hand release types** — Three common follow-through positions: handshake (thumb up, standard hook), palm-up (straight ball), helicopter (rotated over, spinner). The follow-through reveals what the hand did at release. *Introduced: Ch 5.6.*

**Heads** — The first ~20 feet of the lane (from foul line). The "skid zone" where the ball typically slides on heavy oil before encountering friction. *Introduced: Ch 6.1.*

**Helicopter** — A follow-through position where the hand rotates completely over after release, palm down. Produces a high-axis-tilt "spinner" ball motion. *Introduced: Ch 5.6.*

**Hook** — The lateral curve of a bowling ball as it travels down the lane. Caused by friction between the ball's surface and the lane. The "amount" of hook is measured in boards (e.g. "12 boards of hook"). *Introduced: Ch 2.4.*

**House shot** — The standard recreational lane oil pattern. Heavy oil concentrated in the center boards (10-30) creates "walls" that funnel errant shots back to the pocket. Typical ratio: 10:1 (center vs outside). *Introduced: Ch 7.2.*

---

## I

**IDF (Intermediate differential)** — A measure of asymmetry in bowling ball cores. Symmetric cores have IDF below 0.010; asymmetric cores have IDF above 0.010. Affects how the ball flares and where the strongest hook happens. *Introduced: Ch 2.2.*

---

## L

**Lane** — The playing surface, 60 feet long and 41.5 inches wide, with 39 boards. Surfaces are either traditional wood (maple heads + pine midlane) or modern synthetic. *Introduced: Ch 1.1.*

**Lane transition** — The progressive change in oil distribution as games are played. Oil gets removed from the ball track area and pushed down the lane (carrydown). Bowlers adjust their target line to compensate. *Introduced: Ch 7.5.*

**Loft** — The horizontal distance between the foul line and where the ball first contacts the lane after release. Standard loft is 1-2 feet. Heavy loft (5-6 feet) intentionally skips over part of the oil pattern. *Introduced: Ch 6.5.*

---

## M

**Mass bias (MB)** — In asymmetric bowling balls, the location of the highest mass concentration outside the geometric center. Marked with a dot on the ball's surface. *Introduced: Ch 2.6.*

**Messenger** — A pin that flies across the deck after impact and clips another pin (especially the 10-pin), often saving a strike. *Introduced: Ch 8.3.*

**Midlane** — The middle ~20 feet of the lane (from 20 to 40 feet). The "hook zone" where the ball typically transitions from skid to hook. *Introduced: Ch 6.1.*

**Muscled swing** — A swing where the bowler applies arm/shoulder force to the swing path, fighting against gravity. Produces inconsistent ball speed and release. The opposite of a free swing. *Introduced: Ch 4.4.*

---

## O

**Oil pattern** — The distribution of conditioning oil on the lane. Patterns vary by length (32-48 feet), volume (20-30 mL), and ratio (center-vs-outside oil concentration). Two main families: house shots (high ratio, easier) and sport shots (low ratio, harder). *Introduced: Ch 7.1.*

---

## P

**PAP (Positive Axis Point)** — The point on the bowling ball that defines the bowler's natural axis of rotation. Pro shops measure each bowler's PAP to determine the optimal drilling layout. *Introduced: Ch 2.6.*

**PBA patterns** — The 10 official competition oil patterns used by the Professional Bowlers Association, named after animals: Wolf (32ft, shortest), Cheetah (35ft), Viper (36ft), Bat (36ft), Chameleon (39ft), Bear (40ft, flat), Scorpion (42ft), Dragon (45ft), Badger (47ft), Shark (48ft, longest). *Introduced: Ch 7.3.*

**Pearl reactive** — A coverstock type with a pearlescent finish (mica particles in the resin). Tends to skid longer and snap harder at the breakpoint compared to solid reactive. *Introduced: Ch 2.1.*

**Pendulum (swing)** — The arc traced by the ball during a free swing, mathematically similar to a clock's pendulum. The free swing IS a pendulum. *Introduced: Ch 4.4.*

**Pin (drilling marker)** — A small colored dot on the bowling ball surface marking the top of the core. Used as a reference point when drilling. NOT to be confused with the bowling pin (the target). *Introduced: Ch 2.6.*

**Pin (target)** — The 10 white objects you're trying to knock down. Each is 15" tall, weighs 3 lbs 6-10 oz, and falls when tilted past 9-11 degrees. Made of laminated maple wood with a plastic coating. *Introduced: Ch 1.2.*

**Pin action** — How the pins behave after the ball makes contact. The ball directly hits only 4 pins (1, 3, 5, 9 for a right-handed strike); the other 6 fall via chain-reaction collisions. Good pin action means clean strikes; bad pin action means leaves. *Introduced: Ch 8.3.*

**Pin deck** — The flat area at the back of the lane where the 10 pins stand in their triangle formation. Made of harder material than the lane itself to handle pin impacts. *Introduced: Ch 1.2.*

**Plastic ball** — A ball made of polyester (the softest, smoothest coverstock). Goes nearly straight. Used as a "spare ball" for shooting corner pins. *Introduced: Ch 2.1.*

**Pocket** — The gap between pins 1 and 3 (for right-handers) or pins 1 and 2 (for left-handers). The optimal target for a strike. About 7 inches wide. *Introduced: Ch 8.1.*

**Polish** — A ball surface preparation finer than 5000 grit, producing a mirror-smooth finish. Polished balls skid the longest and hook the latest. Compare with sanded. *Introduced: Ch 2.5.*

**Pushaway** — The first motion of the swing where the bowler extends the ball forward from the stance position. Sets up the entire swing path. *Introduced: Ch 4.1.*

## R

**Reactive resin** — A coverstock category that uses a polyurethane base mixed with reactive additives. The most common modern coverstock. Comes in three subtypes: solid (most aggressive on oil), pearl (longer skid, sharper backend), and hybrid (in between). *Introduced: Ch 2.1.*

**Release** — The moment the ball leaves the bowler's hand, ideally at the bottom of the swing arc near the slide foot's ankle. The release sequence (thumb exit, weight transfer to fingers, finger lift) determines the rev rate and axis. *Introduced: Ch 5.1.*

**Rev rate** — Revolutions per minute. How fast the ball spins after release. Typical ranges: stroker 200-300, tweener 300-370, cranker 400-550, two-hander 450-650. Higher rev rate = more hook potential. *Introduced: Ch 5.2 (Rev Rate page is the only existing production content).*

**RG (Radius of gyration)** — A measure of how mass is distributed inside the ball. Low RG (~2.46) = mass near the center = ball revs up quickly = early hook. High RG (~2.80) = mass near the shell = ball revs up slowly = later hook. Like a figure skater pulling arms in (low RG, fast spin) vs out (high RG, slow spin). *Introduced: Ch 2.3.*

**Ringing 10** — A 10-pin leave where the ball entered the pocket with TOO much energy and the 6-pin wrapped around the 10-pin instead of taking it down. Compare with flat 10. *Introduced: Ch 8.5.*

**Roll (phase)** — The third phase of ball motion (after skid and hook), where the ball is rotating end-over-end and traveling in a straight line. Happens after the breakpoint, typically in the last 15-20 feet before the pins. *Introduced: Ch 6.1.*

**Rule of 31** — A formula for finding the breakpoint board on a given oil pattern: `breakpoint board = pattern length - 31`. Example: a 40-foot pattern has its breakpoint around board 9. *Introduced: Ch 11.1.*

---

## S

**Sanded ball** — A ball with a rougher surface preparation (typically 500-2000 grit). Hooks earlier than polished balls. *Introduced: Ch 2.5.*

**Skid (phase)** — The first phase of ball motion. The ball slides on the oil-covered heads (first 20 feet) without yet hooking. *Introduced: Ch 6.1.*

**Slide foot** — The foot that slides at the foul line during the release. For a right-handed bowler, the left foot. The release point is typically at the slide foot's ankle. *Introduced: Ch 3.1.*

**Spare** — Knocking down all 10 pins on the second ball of a frame (after leaving some pins on the first ball). Worth 10 plus the next ball. Spare conversion is the highest-leverage skill in bowling. *Introduced: Ch 9.*

**Speed (ball speed)** — How fast the ball is traveling when it reaches the pins. Typical range: 13-22 mph. Optimal speed for most bowlers: 17-18 mph at the pins. Higher speed = ball deflects less but hooks less. *Introduced: Ch 6.2.*

**Split** — A leave where two or more pins are standing with at least one pin missing between them. Splits are very hard to convert. The 7-10 split is the most famous. *Introduced: Ch 9.4.*

**Sport shot** — A challenging oil pattern with a low ratio (typically under 4:1). Eliminates the "walls" of a house shot, requiring much more precision. PBA tournaments use sport shots. *Introduced: Ch 7.4.*

**Stance** — The starting position of the bowler before beginning the approach. Includes ball height, knee flex, body posture, and starting board. *Introduced: Ch 3.1.*

**Stroker** — A bowling style characterized by smooth, controlled motion and lower rev rate (200-300 RPM). The opposite of a cranker. *Introduced: Ch 5.2.*

**Strike** — Knocking down all 10 pins on the first ball of a frame. Worth 10 plus the next two balls. The goal of every first delivery. *Introduced: Ch 8.6.*

**Synthetic lane** — A modern lane surface made of laminated polymer materials. More durable and consistent than wood. Tends to produce sharper, more angular ball motion. Compare with wood lane. *Introduced: Ch 7.6.*

---

## T

**Three-six-nine (3-6-9) system** — A spare-shooting system where the bowler moves their feet 3, 6, or 9 boards from their strike position to convert specific pins, while keeping the same arrow target. The simplest comprehensive spare system. *Introduced: Ch 9.2.*

**Track area** — The boards (typically 8-12) where most balls travel during a session. The first boards to dry out as oil gets pushed around. *Introduced: Ch 7.5.*

**Track flare** — See Flare.

**Trail leg** — The leg that swings behind the bowler during the slide and release. For a right-handed bowler, the right leg. Usually swings sideways to clear the slide foot's path. *Introduced: Ch 4.3.*

**Tweener** — A bowling style between a stroker and cranker, with moderate rev rate (300-370 RPM) and balanced hook. Most recreational bowlers fall in this range. *Introduced: Ch 5.2.*

**Two-handed** — A modern bowling style where the bowler places their non-dominant hand on the side of the ball during the swing for support, then peels it away just before release. Produces dramatically higher rev rates (450-650 RPM) and more hook than one-handed bowling. *Introduced: Ch 5.5, Ch 12.*

---

## U

**Urethane** — A coverstock type, less aggressive than reactive resin but more than plastic. Hooks smoothly and predictably. Was the dominant coverstock from the 1980s until reactive resin took over in the 1990s. Still used today on dry lane conditions. *Introduced: Ch 2.1.*

**USBC** — United States Bowling Congress. The governing body for bowling in the US. Sets equipment specifications, lane specifications, and competition rules. *Universally referenced.*

---

## W

**Wood lane** — Traditional bowling lane surface made of maple boards in the heads and pine boards in the midlane. Less durable than synthetic, more affected by humidity, but produces a smoother, more arcing ball motion. *Introduced: Ch 7.6.*

**Wrist position** — The angle of the bowler's wrist at release. Three main positions: cupped (wrist bent up, max revs), straight/firm (neutral, balanced), and broken (wrist bent down, weak release). *Introduced: Ch 5.1.*

---

## Numbers

**1-3 pocket** — The gap between pin 1 (head pin) and pin 3 (right side of row 2). The strike target for a right-handed bowler.

**1-2 pocket** — The gap between pin 1 and pin 2. The strike target for a left-handed bowler.

**4-step approach** — The standard bowling approach with 4 walking steps. Right-handers start with the right foot.

**5-step approach** — A variation of the approach with an additional "trigger step" at the beginning. Right-handers start with the left foot.

**6 degrees** — USBC research found that 6 degrees is the optimal entry angle for a strike. At this angle, the strike zone is approximately 3x wider than at 2 degrees.

**7-10 split** — The hardest common split. Pins 7 and 10 standing alone in the back row corners, 36 inches apart with nothing between them. Conversion rate < 1%.

**12 inches** — The center-to-center spacing between adjacent pins in the triangle formation.

**60 feet** — The length of the bowling lane from foul line to head pin.

**41.5 inches** — The width of the bowling lane.

**39 boards** — The number of longitudinal boards making up the lane width.

## How to Add Terms to This Glossary

When a content writing session uses a bowling term that isn't in this glossary:

1. Check the research files for an authoritative definition
2. Add an entry alphabetically with the format above (term, definition, "Introduced: Ch X.Y" cross-reference)
3. Update the "Last Updated" date at the top
4. Mention the addition in the commit message

When a content writing session uses a term that IS in this glossary, the definition in the content should be **consistent** with this glossary. If you find yourself disagreeing with a glossary definition, update the glossary first (with a brief justification in the commit) — then update the content.

The goal: any reader can look up any term and get the same answer regardless of which content section they're reading.
